### Discrite approximation of the gradient
a 1d signal could just be a row of pixels on a image, we can simply measure the change using subtraction in a discrite signal
we can both calculate the backwords and forward differences
![[Pasted image 20261008152739.png]]
**Backwards:**

$\frac{\partial I(x,y)}{\partial x} \cong I_x(i,j) = I(i,j) - I(i,j-1)$

$\frac{\partial I(x,y)}{\partial y} \cong I_y(i,j) = I(i,j) - I(i-1,j)$

**forwards:**

$\frac{\partial I(x,y)}{\partial x} \cong I_x(i,j) = I(i,j+1) - I(i,j)$

$\frac{\partial I(x,y)}{\partial y} \cong I_y(i,j) = I(i+1,j) - I(i,j)$

### edge and noise
In real images an edge will not look as smooth as we have seen (due to noise)  
- Taking derivatives of noisy signals is an ill posed problem…the solution is not robust wrt to input variations => Derivatives amplify noise 
- To work with real images, an edge detector should therefore be robust to noise, so as to highlight the meaningful edges only and filter out effectively the spurious transitions caused by noise 
- How do we get rid of noise?
we can use one of our image filtering algorithms but then we smooths the edges wich is the thing we want to detect
pixels of different intencities will then look similar after the filtering 
Smoothing and differentiation can be carried out jointly within a single step. we can havoid smoothing the edges by calculating the mean perpendicular to the direction of edge detections. To try avoiding smoothing across edges the two operations are carried out along orthogonal directions

### thresholding
detecting edfes by gradient thresholding is inherently inaccurate
it is difficulto to chhose the meaningful edges characterized by different contrast (i.e. "stronger" as well as "weaker")

![[Pasted image 20261008162102.png]]

A better approach to detect edges may consist in finding the local maxima of the absolute value of the derivative of the signal.

### Non-Maxima Suppresion (NMS)

When dealing with images (2D signals) one should look for:
- maxima of the absolute value of the derivative (i.e. the gradient magnitude)
- along the gradient direction (i.e. orthogonal to the edge direction)

![[Pasted image 20261008162803.png]]

we don't know in advance the correct direction to carry out NMS, The direction has to be estimated locally (based on gradient’s direction)
